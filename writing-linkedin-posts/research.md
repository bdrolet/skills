# What the evidence says about LinkedIn posts (researched 2026-10-14)

Almost all of this is vendor and agency analysis of their own data. LinkedIn
publishes no signal weights. Confidence is noted per item.

## Ranking signals

- **Dwell time and substantive comments** are what the feed rewards; likes
  barely count. Multi-sentence comments from people in the field outweigh
  volume. The circulating "comments = 15× likes" figure has no traceable
  source. (Direction: high confidence. Numbers: low.)
- **Early engagement** in the first 60–90 minutes decides whether a post is
  shown beyond the initial 2–5% test audience. Author replies count as
  engagement. (Medium-high.)
- **Topic authority:** 2026 sources describe the model reading a post
  against the author's profile and posting history. One subject, repeatedly,
  beats range. (Medium.)
- **"Generic AI content"** is punished by readers scrolling (≈3 s dwell), not
  by a detector. The fix is one specific insight from direct experience — a
  result, a decision and its reasoning, a pattern from real work. (Medium-high.)

## The link question (unsettled)

- Largest datasets: external link costs 20–36% reach — Ordinal 26.5% median;
  LinkPost (358k posts, 2026) 448 vs 705 median impressions. A 300-post test
  claims ~60%; ContentIn argues the penalty is folk wisdom; one Q1-2026
  analysis found linked posts doing better.
- **Personal profiles show "almost none" of the penalty** (Ordinal); company
  pages take the hit.
- **Link in first comment** "stopped being free around 2024" and costs most
  of the clicks. If the goal is readers, the link goes in the body.

## Mechanics with reasonable agreement

- First ~140 characters visible on mobile before "…see more" (~210 desktop).
  Lead with a concrete detail or open loop; cut throat-clearing. (High.)
- No hashtags, or at most 1–2: posts without them do 5–10% better. (Medium.)
- Text-only beats single-image by ~30%; a link's OG card renders anyway.
- End with a specific question needing a real answer, not "agree?".
- Tue–Thu, 7–9 am in the audience's time zone; avoid 12–2 pm. Weekend posts
  can gain traction by Monday. (Medium.)
- 3–4 quality posts a week is the recommended cadence; quality dropping is
  worse than posting less — the feed can suppress all of an account's posts.
- Document (PDF carousel) posts have the highest engagement (~6.6%) because
  they keep readers on-platform. Candidate format for a cornerstone piece.
- Avoid engagement pods (claimed 97% detection) and engagement bait.
- B2B decision-makers (Edelman–LinkedIn studies) prefer brevity, and poorly
  executed thought leadership actively *lowers* respect for the author.
